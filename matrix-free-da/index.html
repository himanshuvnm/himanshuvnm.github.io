Matrix-Free Covariance-Response for Data Assimilation-Ready Weather Ensembles

A geometric perspective on generative weather ensembles: rather than matching only scalar spread, study how forecast uncertainty responds to observation-space directions.

01 / The motivation

Good spread does not imply useful covariance.

Two forecast ensembles may have the same total variance and yet react very differently when assimilating observations. Data assimilation depends on the directional structure of uncertainty—not just its magnitude.

Scalar spread

How much uncertainty?

Trace-based or average variance statistics summarize the overall scale of ensemble fluctuations, but collapse directional information.

Covariance geometry

Where is uncertainty?

Off-diagonal correlations and anisotropic covariance determine which state-space directions are coupled.

Assimilation response

How will observations act?

The background covariance determines how innovations are propagated through the Kalman gain.

tr(B₁) = tr(B₂)   ⇏   B₁Hᵀ = B₂HᵀEqual total variance does not determine covariance response.
Research question. Can generated ensemble uncertainty be evaluated—and potentially trained—using the covariance responses that matter for data assimilation, without explicitly forming enormous covariance matrices?
02 / Mathematical foundations

The covariance objects that assimilation actually uses.

Ensemble covariance

For centered ensemble anomalies X ∈ ℝn×m with m members:

B = XXᵀ / (m − 1)

In operational settings, n may be enormous, making explicit storage of B impractical.

Kalman gain

For a linearized observation operator H and observation-error covariance R:

K = BHᵀ (HBHᵀ + R)⁻¹

Both cross-covariance response BHᵀ and projected covariance HBHᵀ influence the update.

Bv = X(Xᵀv) / (m − 1)A covariance-vector product can be computed without constructing B.

This matrix-free identity is the central computational opportunity: compare the action of reference and generated ensemble covariances on meaningful probe directions.

03 / Covariance-response viewpoint

Compare actions, not just summaries.

A probe v exposes how the covariance transforms a particular direction. Repeating this with observation-relevant or randomized probes provides a scalable route to testing geometric agreement.

05 / Scientific evaluation

From ensemble calibration to assimilation skill.

A rigorous evaluation distinguishes uncertainty magnitude, covariance geometry, and downstream analysis quality. These are related but not interchangeable.

Ensemble statistics

Spread and reliability

Ensemble spread, ensemble-mean RMSE, and spread-to-error ratios describe calibration but do not fully identify covariance.

Covariance structure

Response and projection

Compare covariance-vector responses, BHᵀ, HBHᵀ, and their sensitivity to observation geometry.

Downstream utility

Analysis behavior

Measure Kalman-gain discrepancy and analysis RMSE in controlled data-assimilation experiments.
